🎯
The Goal
More playlists to pitch to.
More playlists to land on.
Right now, the team pitches what they know. This tool expands that surface — surfacing editorial playlists they weren't thinking about, based on where similar artists have already been placed. The more precise and targeted the pitch, the better our chances of landing. More placements per release means a stronger, more competitive marketing arm for Symphonic overall.
The Problem & The Opportunity

Better intel.
Stronger pitches.
More landings.

Every time a rep preps a pitch, they're manually assembling information from four different places — and still only pitching to the playlists they already know about. This tool solves both problems at once.

⏱ Today's Workflow
1
Manually scan the release schedule to spot which releases have no driver submission yet (drivers = marketing context submitted by the label: press, tours, accolades — used to pitch DSPs)
2
Open Spotify, look up the artist, browse their current playlists
3
Scroll to "Fans also like" — manually open each similar artist, check their playlists
4
Open Chartmetric separately, look up prior editorial placements
5
Open Airtable, find the submission, check what's actually been filled in
6
Now — finally — start thinking about the pitch. Still limited to playlists you already knew.
⚡ With This Tool
1
Open the release in Airtable — missing submissions are already flagged
2
Click the profile link — score, history, and targets load instantly
3
Review the playlist targets — playlists you weren't thinking about, backed by similar artist data
4
Build a more targeted, more informed pitch — and land on more playlists

The Scorecard — Built Together
A number we define as a team.
A benchmark we refine over time.
The score isn't something we're handing down — it's something we build with the marketing team. We bring the framework; the team brings the real-world weighting of what actually predicts a pickup. Simple formulas won't capture the nuances of artist traction — that knowledge lives with the people doing the pitching every day. The result is a shared benchmark: a number that tells reps at a glance how pitch-ready a release is and flags what's missing before the pitch goes out.
Your input is required here. The weighting of these factors — what actually predicts a pickup — can't be finalized without the marketing team's perspective. This meeting is the starting point for that conversation.

The Score as a Report Card
The score's primary job is to help the team understand why a release is or isn't getting pickups. Each release gets its own profile page — a single view of everything needed to evaluate and build a pitch. The score is what ties it all together: a number that reflects the full picture, and a signal for where to focus before sending anything out.
⚖️
The Score is Guidance, Not a Gate
There's no black-and-white rule for what gets pitched. A smaller artist with a lower score can still be the right call if there's a "moment" — Fresh Finds, a viral clip, a tour announcement. The score surfaces context fast; the rep still makes the call.
💬
Question for the Team
These weights are a starting point. We want input on how to rank them — does pickup history matter more than driver quality? Is audience size a real signal when you're deciding whether to pitch? The score should reflect how the team actually thinks, and we expect it to evolve over time.
