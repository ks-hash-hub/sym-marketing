Section 01
Scoring Philosophy

The principles we aligned on in the Jun 25 meeting that will shape every decision below.

Core idea: The score should reflect whether an artist is doing the right things — not just whether they've blown up. Process adherence is more actionable than raw performance.
Process over performance
Did they submit drivers on time? Do they submit for every release? Are they using all available tools? These signal coachable behavior more than raw listener counts do.
Scores must be contextual
10,000 monthly listeners means something very different for an Americana artist than a Pop artist. A raw number with no context can mislead. Genre-weighting and career-stage context are the right direction — we'll gather more input on how to weight on the July 1 call.
Three jobs the score needs to do
Each "bucket" has a distinct audience and use case:
  • Health Check — Client managers coaching artists: are they doing what they're supposed to?
  • Pitch Ammunition — Tailor pitch bullets per DSP using platform-specific metrics (Amazon Voice Index, Spotify ML, growth rates)
  • Opportunities — Identify high-performing artists for relationship building, advances, feature opportunities
Benchmarking approach — TBD
Open question: do we score against an absolute benchmark (e.g. 1M listeners = 100pts) or relative to the rest of our catalog? Relative scoring surfaces who's performing well for us, but makes scores harder to explain to clients. Absolute scoring is simpler to communicate but may skew heavily by genre. This needs a decision on July 1.

Score Pillars

Current 6-pillar plan. Weights are a starting point — final calibration pending July 1 team input.

Total: 100 points across 6 dimensions. The top 3 pillars (Pickups, Audience, Social) are performance signals. The bottom 3 (Story, Drive, Consistency) are process signals. The team leaned toward weighting process more heavily — exact weights TBD.
